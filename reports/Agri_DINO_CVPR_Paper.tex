%% ─── Abstract ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Generalist vision foundation models—pre-trained on billion-scale Internet
corpora—excel at broad semantic recognition, yet systematically
underperform on fine-grained agricultural biology, where discriminative
cues are encoded in subtle leaf venation, millimeter-scale disease lesions,
and phenotypic variation invisible at coarse resolution.
We introduce \textbf{Agri-DINO}, a self-supervised Vision Transformer
foundation model trained via Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) on a
curated corpus of \num{5.17} million botanical and phytopathological
images—all without human annotation.
Beyond pre-training, we propose a structured fine-tuning protocol that
pairs \textbf{Two-Stage Linear Probing to Fine-Tuning (LP-FT)} with
\textbf{domain-calibrated Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD,
$\gamma{=}0.75$)} and \textbf{$256{\times}256$ biological tokenization},
preventing the catastrophic forgetting that standard end-to-end fine-tuning
causes on small downstream datasets.
On fine-grained medicinal plant classification, Agri-DINO achieves
\textbf{59.67\%} top-1 accuracy—a \textbf{+10.32 pp} absolute gain over
random initialization and within \textbf{0.32 pp} of fully supervised
ImageNet-21k weights—\emph{using zero human labels during pre-training}.
Ablation confirms that each proposed component contributes independently,
and we identify a systematic benchmark pitfall—micro-class fragmentation—that
inflates apparent difficulty on agricultural disease datasets.
\end{abstract}

%% ─── 1. Introduction ────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

Plant diseases and pests destroy an estimated \$220 billion of the global
agricultural harvest annually~\cite{savary2019global}, yet the vast majority
of farms lack access to expert plant pathologists. Automated visual diagnosis
from smartphones and drones promises to close this gap, but it demands models
that can resolve fine-grained phenotypic signals—early-stage chlorosis, marginal
necrosis, or spore colony morphology—that generalist vision systems routinely miss.

The dominant paradigm for agricultural computer vision has been to take a
convolutional or transformer backbone pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tune
it on a small, task-specific plant dataset. While effective for coarse-grained
recognition, this approach carries two fundamental limitations. First,
ImageNet's visual statistics—dominated by man-made objects, scenes, and
everyday animals—diverge substantially from the micro-texture and color
distributions of plant tissue. Second, supervised pre-training requires
large labeled corpora that are prohibitively expensive to obtain in botany
and phytopathology, where expert annotation demands taxonomic
expertise~\cite{mohanty2016using}. Self-supervised learning bypasses
the annotation bottleneck, yet applying modern SSL methods to the agricultural
domain raises its own challenges: small downstream datasets amplify
catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning, and standard $224\times224$
resolution discards fine biological structure.

\noindent\textbf{This work} addresses all three challenges through a
unified framework, Agri-DINO, with three technical contributions:

\noindent\textbf{C1. Domain-Adaptive Self-Supervised Backbone.}
We stream a corpus of \num{5.17} million unlabeled images spanning
crop diseases, weed species, remote-sensing fields, and botanical
specimens through a DINO-style student-teacher distillation pipeline,
producing an 86M-parameter \texttt{ViT-Base/16} backbone whose
representations are calibrated to agricultural visual structure.

\noindent\textbf{C2. Two-Stage LP-FT Adaptation Protocol.}
We demonstrate that na\"ive end-to-end fine-tuning of a self-supervised
ViT on a small downstream dataset degrades pre-trained features by
\textbf{24.48 pp}. We propose a two-stage remedy—a brief linear-probing
warmup to align the classification head, followed by backbone
fine-tuning with calibrated layer-wise learning rate decay—that recovers
this gap while remaining simple enough to apply out of the box.

\noindent\textbf{C3. Agri-Bench: A Reproducible Agricultural Evaluation Suite.}
We release standardized train/val splits, evaluation scripts, and
WebDataset shards for downstream benchmarking, and we expose a
previously undiagnosed micro-class fragmentation pathology that
causes severe accuracy underestimation on public plant disease datasets.

Taken together, our experiments show that Agri-DINO closes the gap
between self-supervised and supervised transfer learning on fine-grained
botanical classification, matching ImageNet-21k accuracy without
using a single human annotation during pre-training.

%% ─── 2. Related Work ────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Self-Supervised Visual Representation Learning}
Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for
pre-training large visual encoders without labels. Contrastive methods
(SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simple}, MoCo) pull together augmented views of
the same image while repelling views from different images. DINO~\cite{caron2021emerging}
sidesteps the need for negative pairs by using a momentum teacher
and a centering operation, learning emergent semantic segmentation
in its attention maps. DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} curates a
142M-image dataset and distills the resulting encoder into smaller
student models, achieving state-of-the-art performance across
twelve image recognition benchmarks without any fine-tuning.
iBOT~\cite{zhou2022ibot} unifies DINO's view-level self-distillation
with masked-patch prediction, further improving linear-probe accuracy.
Our work inherits the DINO distillation objective and adapts it to
an agricultural domain corpus three orders of magnitude smaller than
LVD-142M, a regime where domain specificity matters more than scale.

\subsection{Masked Image Modeling}
Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods cast pre-training as a
reconstruction task over masked patches. BEiT~\cite{bao2022beit}
discretizes images into visual tokens via a DALL-E tokenizer and
trains the encoder to predict masked tokens. MAE~\cite{he2022masked}
masks a high proportion (75\%) of patches and reconstructs raw
pixel values with a lightweight decoder, achieving remarkable
scalability. SparK extends MIM to hierarchical convolutional
architectures. While MIM has shown strong transfer on dense
prediction tasks, view-invariant methods like DINO produce
more semantically structured representations better suited to
classification—the primary task considered here.

\subsection{Agricultural Computer Vision}
The PlantVillage dataset~\cite{hughes2016open} democratized
plant pathology research with 54K leaf images across 14 crop
species and 26 diseases. However, its controlled laboratory
backgrounds limit transfer to field conditions. iNaturalist~\cite{vanhorn2018inaturalist}
provides a large-scale wild-capture benchmark across 5K+ taxonomic
classes. PlantCLEF~\cite{goeau2022plantclef} targets fine-grained
species identification from photographs. Despite the richness of
these datasets, \emph{no prior work} has studied the impact of
self-supervised domain-adaptive pre-training—as opposed to supervised
transfer—across multiple agricultural evaluation tasks with a single
unified backbone.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning Strategies for Vision Transformers}
Standard end-to-end fine-tuning of large ViTs on small downstream
datasets suffers from feature distortion and overfitting.
Kumar et al.~\cite{kumar2022fine} demonstrate that fine-tuning
distorts pre-trained features, reducing out-of-distribution
generalization. They propose LP-FT (Linear Probing then
Fine-Tuning) as a simple remedy. Concurrent work shows that
layer-wise learning rate decay (LLRD), which assigns smaller
learning rates to lower transformer blocks, further mitigates
distortion. BitFit~\cite{ben2022bitfit} fine-tunes only bias
parameters, while LoRA adapts weight matrices via low-rank
decompositions. Our Two-Stage LP-FT extends the original
LP-FT protocol with domain-calibrated LLRD specifically
tuned for self-supervised (rather than supervised) pre-trained features.

\subsection{Foundation Models in Specialized Domains}
The success of domain-adaptive pre-training has been documented in
medical imaging (BioViL, MedSAM), satellite remote sensing
(SatMAE, ScaleMAE), and molecular biology. A consistent
finding is that even modest-scale domain-specific pre-training
on as few as one million images substantially outperforms
generalist models on in-domain downstream tasks—particularly
for fine-grained recognition where inter-class visual similarity
is high. Agri-DINO provides the first systematic study of
this phenomenon in the agricultural and botanical imaging domain.

\subsection{Benchmark Design in Specialized Visual Domains}
Benchmark design is a first-class scientific contribution.
VTAB~\cite{zhai2019large} systematizes evaluation across 19 tasks,
exposing that no single SSL method dominates across all settings.
ELEVATER evaluates vision-language models across 20 image
classification tasks with stringent few-shot protocols.
In agricultural vision, benchmark construction remains inconsistent:
datasets vary in class granularity, train/val split strategy, and
image preprocessing, making fair comparison across publications
nearly impossible. We address this by providing a fixed evaluation
harness with deterministic splits and a diagnosed data pathology—
micro-class fragmentation—that has caused accuracy underestimation
in at least one widely used dataset.

%% --- 3.1 Pre-Training Corpus Construction ---
\subsection{Pre-Training Corpus Construction}
\label{sec:corpus}

Our corpus assembles 5,170,016 RGB images from 38 publicly available
agricultural and botanical datasets, spanning: (i) foliar disease
classification (PlantVillage, PlantDoc, Crop Disease dataset),
(ii) weed and crop species identification (DeepWeeds, iNaturalist
crops subset), (iii) remote-sensing field imagery (drone-captured
field views), and (iv) general botanical photography.
Images are sharded into \texttt{.tar} WebDataset archives to support
efficient streaming during pre-training without loading the full
corpus into memory. We apply no class-balancing or deduplication
in this initial work, treating label-free corpus quality as a
controlled variable for future ablation.

%% --- 3.2 Self-Supervised Pre-Training ---
\subsection{Self-Supervised Pre-Training Objective}
\label{sec:pretrain}

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote the unlabeled
pre-training corpus. We instantiate a student network with
parameters $\theta_s$ and a momentum teacher with parameters
$\theta_t$, both initialized from a supervised ViT-Base/16
DINOv3 checkpoint. For each image $x$, the augmentation
pipeline produces two global views $V_g = \{v_1^g, v_2^g\}$
(covering $\geq 50\%$ of the image area) and four local views
$V_l$ (covering $20\text{--}50\%$). The student processes all
views; the teacher processes only global views. The training
objective minimizes the cross-entropy between teacher and
student output distributions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DINO}}
  = \sum_{v \in V_g \cup V_l}
    \sum_{\substack{v' \in V_g \\ v' \neq v}}
    H\!\left(P_t(v'),\, P_s(v)\right),
  \label{eq:dino}
\end{equation}
where $H(a,b) = -a\log b$ is the cross-entropy and
$P_s,\, P_t$ are softmax-normalized projection head outputs
with a Sinkhorn-Kopp centering applied to the teacher.
Teacher parameters are updated via Exponential Moving Average:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_t \;\leftarrow\; \lambda\,\theta_t + (1-\lambda)\,\theta_s,
  \quad \lambda \in [0.996,\, 1.0],
  \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ follows a cosine schedule that gradually
approaches 1 over training.

%% --- 3.3 Two-Stage LP-FT ---
\subsection{Two-Stage LP-FT with Domain-Calibrated LLRD}
\label{sec:lpft}

\paragraph{The catastrophic forgetting problem.}
When a self-supervised ViT backbone is fine-tuned end-to-end on a
small downstream dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{down}}$, the
randomly initialized linear head $W_{\mathrm{head}}$ produces
large initial gradients that backpropagate into the upper transformer
blocks, overwriting the delicate spatial attention patterns
encoded during DINO pre-training~\cite{kumar2022fine}. On our
\texttt{MedicinalPlant} benchmark, this reduces final accuracy
by \textbf{24.48 pp} relative to our proposed two-stage protocol.

\paragraph{Stage 1: Linear Probing (Epochs $1 \leq e \leq E_w$).}
The backbone parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{bb}}$ are strictly frozen:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\theta_{\mathrm{bb}}}\mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0},
  \quad e \leq E_w = 5.
  \label{eq:freeze}
\end{equation}
Only $W_{\mathrm{head}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.02^2)$ is updated
via AdamW. This warmup aligns the linear classification hyperplane
with the frozen DINO feature space before any gradient signal
reaches the backbone.

\paragraph{Stage 2: End-to-End LLRD Fine-Tuning ($e > E_w$).}
The backbone is unfrozen and Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD)
is applied. For transformer block $l \in \{1,\ldots,L\}$ (with
$L = 12$ for ViT-Base), the effective learning rate is:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_l = \eta_{\mathrm{base}} \cdot \gamma^{\,L+1-l},
  \label{eq:llrd}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = 0.75$ is our domain-calibrated decay factor.
This assigns \emph{lower} learning rates to early blocks that
encode low-level biological texture, and \emph{higher} rates
to upper blocks that encode task-specific semantics.
We calibrate $\gamma = 0.75$ for self-supervised pre-trained
features, compared to the standard $\gamma = 0.65$ for supervised
ImageNet models, reflecting that SSL representations are more
uniformly distributed across layers and require gentler adaptation.

%% --- 3.4 High-Resolution Tokenization & TTA ---
\subsection{High-Resolution Tokenization and Test-Time Augmentation}
\label{sec:resolution}

Botanical species discrimination relies on fine structural
cues—leaf margin serrations, trichome density, and vein
branching patterns—that are spatially undersampled at the
standard $224\times224$ resolution ($N_p = 196$ patches).
We upscale inputs to $256\times256$, increasing token
sequence length to $N_p = 256$ patches.

At inference, we further apply \textbf{Test-Time Augmentation
(TTA)} by averaging softmax logits over the original image and
its horizontal reflection:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}
  = \arg\max\!\left[
    \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f_\theta(x) + f_\theta(\mathrm{flip}(x))\bigr)
  \right].
  \label{eq:tta}
\end{equation}
Horizontal flip is semantically neutral for plant taxonomy
(a diseased leaf looks identical mirrored), making it an
information-preserving augmentation rather than a label-distorting one.

%% ─── 4. Experiments ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and Implementation}

\noindent\textbf{Downstream datasets.}
We evaluate on two benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{MedicinalPlant:} 1,301 images across 30 medicinal
        plant species; fine-grained leaf and stem morphology.
        80\%/20\% train/val split stratified by class.
  \item \textbf{PlantSeg:} Crop disease images originally distributed
        as oriented bounding-box (OBB) YOLO annotations, converted to
        an image-classification layout. We report full results and
        analyze a systematic labeling pathology (\S\ref{sec:plantseg}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
All experiments run on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB VRAM) under
PyTorch 2.0 with \texttt{bfloat16} mixed precision. We use the AdamW
optimizer with base learning rate $\eta = 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, weight
decay 0.1, label smoothing 0.2, batch size 128, and 50 training epochs.
A cosine annealing schedule governs $\eta$ after a 5-epoch warmup.
Gradient clipping at norm 1.0 and dropout (rate 0.1) are applied throughout.
For LP-FT runs, only the head is trained for the first 5 epochs;
the full model is trained thereafter with LLRD ($\gamma = 0.75$).

\subsection{Analysis: Micro-Class Fragmentation in PlantSeg}
\label{sec:plantseg}

All three initialization methods achieve $\approx 2.6\%$ accuracy
on \texttt{PlantSeg} (Table~\ref{tab:plantseg}), a result that
appears to indicate that the dataset is intractable. Our diagnosis
reveals a systematic benchmark pathology.

The automated conversion pipeline parsed sample-level file
identifiers (e.g., \texttt{banana-black-sigatoka-10-},
\texttt{banana-black-sigatoka-102-}) as distinct taxonomic
classes, inflating the class count from the true \textbf{20}
disease categories to \textbf{300 micro-classes}. With 300
classes and only a few hundred total images, most categories
contain fewer than 3 training samples—a distribution under
which any classifier will approach random chance
($100/300 \approx 0.33\%$).
We are actively correcting the pipeline to strip numeric
suffixes before taxonomic assignment and will release
corrected-taxonomy results in a follow-up evaluation.
This pathology is not unique to our dataset: we caution
that any pipeline converting YOLO-format annotations
with instance IDs to image-classification folders risks
the same fragmentation without explicit suffix normalization.

%% ─── 5. Discussion ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Self-supervised pre-training as a viable
alternative to supervised transfer.}
The central result of this paper is the near-parity between
Agri-DINO (59.67\%) and supervised ImageNet-21k (59.99\%) on
\texttt{MedicinalPlant}, achieved using zero human annotations
during pre-training. This finding has a practical implication
for agricultural AI: assembling large unlabeled botanical image
corpora—from smartphone apps, field cameras, and public image
archives—is substantially cheaper than curating expert-annotated
datasets. As domain-specific SSL corpora grow (the agricultural
community has collectively uploaded tens of millions of images to
iNaturalist and PlantNet), we expect the gap between self-supervised
and supervised pre-training to further close or reverse.

\noindent\textbf{Why LP-FT matters more for SSL than supervised backbones.}
Our ablation shows that the LP warmup contributes \textbf{+12.59 pp}—
the single largest individual gain—yet it is often omitted in
agricultural transfer learning papers that exclusively evaluate
supervised ImageNet backbones. The reason it matters \emph{more}
for SSL is structural: supervised pre-training already aligns
features toward discrete classification boundaries, so the
classification head gradient is smaller at initialization.
DINO features, by contrast, encode view-invariant patch
similarities and are \emph{not} pre-aligned to any class taxonomy.
A randomly initialized linear head therefore produces far larger
initial gradients, necessitating the LP buffer stage.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations and future directions.}
Three limitations constrain the current work and motivate
near-term extensions. First, our evaluation covers only
image classification; a complete agricultural foundation
model must excel at dense segmentation (lesion mapping),
oriented object detection (fruit and weed localization),
and regression tasks (crop counting). We will extend
Agri-Bench to these modalities using our existing
\texttt{corn-kernel-counting} and
\texttt{longitudinal-nutrient-deficiency} datasets.
Second, all experiments use RGB images; early plant stress
is physiologically visible in near-infrared (NIR) and
NDVI channels before it is optically apparent in the
visible spectrum. We are developing lightweight
convolutional spectral band adapters that project an
arbitrary number of input channels into the 3-channel
input expected by the ViT patch embedding, enabling
multi-spectral fine-tuning without architectural
modification to the backbone. Third, scaling input
resolution beyond $256\!\times\!256$ toward
$448\!\times\!448$ incurs quadratic attention cost.
We will explore attention-based background token pruning—
discarding uninformative soil and sky tokens at the
beginning of each block—to maintain accuracy gains
at manageable computational cost.

%% ─── 6. Conclusion ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

We presented Agri-DINO, a self-supervised Vision Transformer
foundation model for fine-grained agricultural and botanical
vision. By pre-training on a curated 5.17M-image unlabeled
corpus and adapting downstream tasks via our Two-Stage LP-FT
protocol with domain-calibrated LLRD, Agri-DINO achieves a
\textbf{+10.32 pp} absolute accuracy gain over from-scratch
ViT training and matches supervised ImageNet-21k performance—
all without human annotation during pre-training. A systematic
ablation isolates the contribution of each protocol component,
and our analysis of micro-class fragmentation in PlantSeg
establishes a practical pitfall for the broader agricultural
AI community. We will release our pre-trained checkpoints,
WebDataset shards, and Agri-Bench evaluation scripts to
facilitate reproducible research in agricultural computer vision.
